# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary users are clinic administrators managing day-to-day operations: patient records, therapist assignments, session logs, supply stock, and clinical reports. A secondary user type (terapeuta) accesses a scoped view: their own patients and sessions. Both groups are healthcare practitioners working in a clinical context — they need confidence and speed, not novelty.

## Product Purpose

KaironTherapy is a clinical management platform for therapy clinics. It centralizes patient records (fichas clínicas), session tracking, therapist scheduling, supply inventory, and administrative reporting. Success means a clinic admin can move through their daily ops without friction, and a therapist can log a session in under a minute.

## Brand Personality

Caring, grounded, reliable. The product operates in a healthcare context where practitioners carry real responsibility. The UI should feel calm and trustworthy — like a well-organized clinic, not a startup dashboard. Warm without being soft. Professional without being cold.

## Anti-references

- **Hospital EHR systems (Epic, Meditech, Clínica UC)**: avoid the dense gray UI, tiny form fields, overwhelming data tables, and legacy-software aesthetic that feels like a form nightmare. This should feel modern and human.
- **Consumer wellness apps (Headspace, Calm)**: avoid pastels, oversized whitespace, and the "spa app" softness. This is a professional tool, not a consumer product.
- **Generic SaaS (Notion, Linear)**: avoid the ultra-minimal, all-white, heavy-blue-accent look that signals "tech startup" rather than "clinical care."

## Design Principles

1. **Trust through clarity** — every screen should reduce cognitive load. If a practitioner has to think about the UI, it has failed. Prioritize scannable hierarchy over visual decoration.
2. **Warmth without softness** — the deep teal brand is grounded and human. Colors, spacing, and type should feel considered, not clinical gray or consumer pastel.
3. **Reliability over novelty** — predictable patterns and consistent interactions. Therapists and admins rely on this tool during patient care; UI surprises are a real cost.
4. **Data at the right density** — clinical work is information-dense. The UI must organize complexity without hiding it behind excessive whitespace or forcing extra clicks to surface key data.
5. **Human at the center** — patients are people, not rows. The interface should reflect that context through respectful tone, clear labeling, and friction-reducing flows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance minimum. Ensure sufficient contrast for body text and form labels (≥4.5:1). Support keyboard navigation for all interactive elements. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any animations. No color-only status indicators — always pair color with text or icon.
